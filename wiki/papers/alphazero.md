# Mastering Chess and Shogi by Self-Play with a General Reinforcement Learning Algorithm

> **Short name:** `alphazero` · **arXiv:** [1712.01815](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01815) · **PDF:** [local](../../papers/alphazero_1712.01815.pdf) · **Date:** 2017-12 (preprint) / 2018 (Science) · **Venue:** Science 362(6419):1140-1144 (journal version titled *"A general reinforcement learning algorithm that masters chess, shogi, and Go through self-play"*)

**Authors:** David Silver, Thomas Hubert, Julian Schrittwieser, Ioannis Antonoglou, Matthew Lai, Arthur Guez et al. (DeepMind)

## Abstract
AlphaZero generalizes AlphaGo Zero's self-play tabula-rasa training
recipe from Go to a single algorithm that masters chess, shogi, and
Go from random play, given only the rules of the game. A single deep
neural network outputs a policy over moves and a value estimate; a
Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) uses both during play; self-play games
generated by the MCTS produce the training data used to update the
network. Within 24 hours of training (on the compute scale described
in the paper), AlphaZero reached superhuman level in all three games
and defeated the world-champion programs Stockfish (chess), Elmo
(shogi), and a previous version of AlphaGo (Go). No domain-specific
adaptations, no human data beyond the game rules, and no handcrafted
evaluation functions are used.

## Key contributions
- Demonstrates that a *single* reinforcement learning algorithm —
  policy iteration through MCTS self-play — can reach superhuman
  performance across three very different games with no
  game-specific engineering.
- Formalises the self-play coevolutionary loop as a specific instance
  of policy iteration: the MCTS acts as a *policy improvement*
  operator, generating better-than-policy moves; self-play data
  trains the network to match those moves; the improved network
  strengthens the MCTS in the next round.
- Shows that *dense* domain knowledge (opening books, handcrafted
  evaluation, alpha-beta search) is not required to beat decades of
  specialised engineering on chess, shogi, or Go — a point
  decisively made against a 20-year consensus in computer chess.
- Establishes the *tabula-rasa* self-play template that underlies
  most subsequent competitive game-AI work, including MuZero (2020)
  and extensions into imperfect-information games.

## Method at a glance
One neural network `f_θ(s) → (π(s), v(s))` maps a game state to a
move-probability distribution `π` and a scalar value `v ∈ [-1, 1]`.
At play time, MCTS uses `π` as a prior for tree expansion and `v` as
the leaf evaluation; the search returns an improved move distribution
`π_MCTS` that is stronger than the raw network policy. During
training, the agent plays itself — each self-play game yields
trajectories `(s_t, π_MCTS(s_t), z)` where `z` is the game outcome.
The network is updated by minimizing `(z − v)² + H(π_MCTS, π)` across
the self-play corpus. Each generation the network gets stronger, the
MCTS gets stronger, and self-play produces harder training examples —
an [automatic curriculum](../concepts/automatic-curriculum.md) that
emerges from the loop without any external difficulty schedule.

## Why it matters
AlphaZero is the canonical reference for **self-play coevolution at
scale** — the degenerate case of [coevolution](../concepts/coevolution.md)
where one population plays against itself. It is also the paper
everyone points at for "the environment that generates training data
is the system itself": the curriculum, the opponents, and the data
are all produced by the self-play loop, not by external supervision.
For researchers interested in [automatic curricula](../concepts/automatic-curriculum.md)
and in learning from automatically generated environments, AlphaZero
is the single most impactful demonstration. The *"equivalent of
Transformer"* for self-play RL, if such a thing exists.

## Strengths
- The single-algorithm-many-domains result is decisive. Same
  hyperparameters, same architecture, same training pipeline — only
  the game rules change. This is the empirical case for generality
  that no prior game-AI work had delivered.
- The self-play curriculum is automatic and unbounded within the
  domain's difficulty ceiling: neither the training data nor the
  opponent schedule is hand-designed. Exactly the "environment
  creates data" dynamic many practitioners want.
- Reproducible recipe: the released pseudocode, later extended into
  open-source re-implementations (Leela Chess Zero, KataGo, ELF
  OpenGo), has made AlphaZero one of the most-replicated deep-RL
  systems ever published.
- Sets a benchmark for *compute-scaling*: subsequent work (MuZero,
  AlphaStar, AlphaCode, AlphaFold's self-distillation variants) is
  clearly descended from the AlphaZero policy-iteration structure
  plus a planning or search component.

## Limitations and open critiques
- **Perfect-information, two-player, zero-sum games only.** The
  algorithm as written does not extend to imperfect-information
  games (poker, Starcraft) or cooperative settings. Subsequent
  work — Player-of-Games (Schmid et al. 2021) for imperfect info,
  and Cicero for Diplomacy — addresses this but is architecturally
  distinct.
- **Massive compute.** The paper reports training on thousands of
  TPUs. While the algorithm itself is simple, reproducing the
  published results is out of reach for most researchers. Later
  open-source replicas (KataGo in particular) showed the compute
  bound could be reduced substantially, but the original paper does
  not establish a sample-efficient recipe.
- **Degenerate coevolution.** Self-play has the cleanest possible
  coevolutionary dynamics because the "adversary" is literally a
  copy of the agent. Many of the classical coevolution pathologies
  (disengagement, mediocre stable states) do not apply as sharply in
  zero-sum perfect-information games. Claims about AlphaZero-style
  self-play generalising to *open*, multi-agent, or non-zero-sum
  settings should not be read back from this paper.
- **Environment is fixed.** AlphaZero evolves the *policy*; the
  environment (the game rules) is constant. For "environment
  creates data" use cases where the environment itself should
  evolve, AlphaZero is an inspiration but not a direct template —
  see [POET](../concepts/coevolution.md) and the broader
  [unsupervised-environment-design](../concepts/unsupervised-environment-design.md)
  thread.

## Follow-up work and dialogue
AlphaZero's direct lineage continues through MuZero (Schrittwieser
et al., Nature 2020) — same policy-iteration-via-search structure,
but with a *learned* world model instead of explicit game rules,
making the approach applicable to Atari and other domains where
perfect simulators are expensive. EfficientZero (Ye et al. 2021)
pushes MuZero's sample efficiency to orders-of-magnitude lower.
Stochastic MuZero (Antonoglou et al. 2022) handles stochastic
environments. AlphaDev (Mankowitz et al. 2023) applied the same
recipe to program synthesis for sorting algorithms. On the
coevolution side, AlphaZero is the direct ancestor of every
self-play result in the modern deep-RL era, including the policy
co-training in LLM post-training (RLHF / DPO lineage) that
implicitly uses reward-model self-play dynamics. The most relevant
evolutionary follow-up in this wiki is [PAIRED](./paired.md), which
keeps the self-play adversarial structure but externalises the
adversary into an environment generator — a generalization of
AlphaZero's fixed-environment self-play to an environment-generating
self-play.

## Reproducibility
- **Open code:** not officially by DeepMind. The paper publishes
  pseudocode; high-quality open-source replicas exist (Leela Chess
  Zero, KataGo, ELF OpenGo).
- **Domains used:** chess, shogi, Go.
- **Compute disclosed:** 5000 first-generation Tensor Processing
  Units (TPUs) for generating self-play games, 64 second-generation
  TPUs for training. Training time ~24 hours for superhuman play in
  each game; no total FLOP count disclosed in the preprint.
- **Hyperparameters:** shared architecture (20-block / 40-block
  residual ConvNet with policy + value heads), MCTS simulation count
  800 per move during training, Dirichlet noise added to root priors
  for exploration, single set of hyperparameters across all three
  games.

## When to cite this paper
Cite AlphaZero as the canonical reference for *self-play
coevolution as a policy-iteration algorithm* — neural network +
MCTS + self-play update, single recipe across multiple games. It
is the right citation for "self-play generates an automatic
curriculum", "planning-augmented RL", and "deep RL without human
data". For applications in imperfect-information games, cite
Player-of-Games instead; for efficient compute or learned world
models, cite MuZero.
